This guide is written for **UX designers, product designers, and design researchers** who want to prototype more quickly and test ideas earlier in the design process. If you've ever felt bottlenecked waiting for developer time to see your design come to life — or wished you could validate an interaction pattern before a single line of production code was written — this guide is for you. The focus is practical and hands-on: real tools, real workflows, and real prototypes you can share with stakeholders and users.

---

## Ideal Readers

This guide is a great fit if you are:

- **A UX or UI designer** who wants to move beyond static wireframes and clickable mockups into fully interactive, code-based prototypes — without needing to become a developer.
- **A product designer** who collaborates closely with engineers and wants to better understand the technical side, speak their language, and reduce friction in the handoff process.
- **A design lead or design manager** who needs to quickly evaluate the feasibility of a design direction before committing team resources to a full build.
- **A design researcher** who wants to build lightweight, testable interfaces to run usability studies — without depending on another team's sprint cycle.
- **A design student** who wants to learn a modern, AI-augmented workflow that's increasingly valued in industry hiring.

---

## Prerequisites

No coding experience is required to follow this guide — but here's what will set you up for the best experience:

- **A design tool background** — Familiarity with tools like Figma, Sketch, or Adobe XD means you'll already think in terms of components, layouts, and user flows, which translates directly to writing good AI prompts.
- **A GitHub account** — It's free to create at [github.com](https://github.com). The guide walks you through everything you need to know to get started.
- **An AI assistant subscription** — Several options are available at different price points (including free tiers). The [Setup guide](/getting-started/setup) covers your options and how to choose the right one for your workflow.

That's it. Everything else, you'll learn here.

<Warning>
  AI-generated prototypes are designed for **testing and validation** — not for shipping directly to production without developer review. Treat them as high-fidelity design artifacts, not finished products. Always involve your engineering team before any prototype code goes anywhere near real users in a live environment.
</Warning>

---

## What You'll Be Able to Do

After completing this guide, you will be able to:

- **Generate interactive UI prototypes** from plain-language descriptions of your design, without writing code by hand.
- **Prompt AI assistants effectively** for UX-specific tasks — components, flows, interactions, responsive layouts, and accessibility considerations.
- **Manage prototype versions with GitHub** — save your work, track iterations, and share specific versions with teammates or stakeholders.
- **Share prototypes instantly** with a link, enabling faster feedback cycles from stakeholders, developers, and research participants.

## Not Sure If This Is For You?

If you're reading this thinking _"I've never touched code in my life — is this really for me?"_, the answer is: **yes, absolutely.** This guide was written with that reader in mind. Every concept is explained from first principles, every tool is introduced step by step, and no prior technical knowledge is assumed. AI prototyping is one of the most accessible on-ramps into the technical side of product development that has ever existed for designers — and this guide is designed to make the most of that opportunity.
